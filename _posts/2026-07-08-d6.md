It's a die. It's a grid. You roll the die around the grid.

The die doesn't slide. It tumbles. Every move changes which face is up, which
face is down, and which faces are hiding on the sides.
You're not moving a piece. You're moving all six numbers at once.

## The job

Every **task tile** wants one specific face. `TOP 5` wants a five staring at the
sky when you land on it. `BTM 3` wants a three pressed into the floor where you
can't see it. Clear all of them, the **goal** lights up, and then you roll onto it.

## The tiles

- **Conveyor:** slides you one cell over. Faces stay exactly as they were. Free
  movement, zero rotation, which is either the answer or the problem.
- **Spin:** turns the die in place. Same top, same bottom, completely different
  sides.
- **Portal:** paired. In one, out the other, orientation untouched. Solves
  distance.

They chain, too. Line them up wrong and you'll conveyor into a portal into
another conveyor and end up three tiles from where you meant to be, wrong side
up, wondering what you did to deserve it.

## The view

The camera looks straight down a grid axis. That's deliberate: it means two of
the die's six faces are edge-on and completely invisible, and no amount of
squinting will tell you what's on them.

**SPACE** swings the view a quarter turn around the die. The die stays put and
the board rotates underneath it. If you've lost track of what's on the left,
go and look — it costs you nothing but the time.

## A new one every day

There's a fresh puzzle every morning, and nobody designs it. A generator builds
a board, then solves it exhaustively — every reachable combination of position,
orientation and cleared tasks — and throws it away unless it's genuinely worth
playing: the special tiles have to matter, the tasks have to cost you a detour,
and the difficulty has to come from the die rather than the walking. Whatever
survives gets published. It's always solvable, because the thing that publishes
it has already solved it.

Mondays are small. Sundays are not.

## Medals

Because the generator knows the shortest solution that exists, it can tell you
exactly how close you came.

Solve today's puzzle once and the target appears: **par**, your best, and a
coloured disc beside the move counter on every run after that. The disc starts
platinum and only ever falls. Platinum means you matched the shortest route
that exists — that one's exact. Gold, silver and bronze widen out generously
from there; you'd have to take three times the optimal route to come away with
nothing. It updates as you play, so you get to watch it slip the moment you
decide to take the scenic route.

The first run is deliberately clean. No par, no medal, no clock you're being
judged against. Just the puzzle.
