It's a die. It's a grid. You roll the die around the grid. How hard could it be?

The die doesn't slide. It tumbles. Every move changes which face is up, which
face is down, and which faces are hiding on the sides waiting to ruin your plan.
You're not moving a piece. You're moving all six numbers at once.

## The job

Every **task tile** wants one specific face. `TOP 5` wants a five staring at the
sky when you land on it. `BTM 3` wants a three pressed into the floor where you
can't see it. Clear all of them, the **goal** lights up, and then — only then —
you roll onto it.

Yes, you have to keep track of the bottom face. No, the game will not show it to
you. That's the entire puzzle.

## The tiles that hate you

- **Conveyor:** slides you one cell over. Faces stay exactly as they were. Free
  movement, zero rotation, which is either the answer or the problem.
- **Spin:** turns the die in place. Same top, same bottom, completely different
  sides. Wonderful.
- **Portal:** paired. In one, out the other, orientation untouched. Solves
  distance. Does nothing for your face problem.

They chain, too. Line them up wrong and you'll conveyor into a portal into
another conveyor and end up three tiles from where you meant to be, wrong side
up, wondering what you did to deserve it.

## Under the hood

Vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No build step. No framework. No dependencies.
No `node_modules` sitting on the disk like a second operating system.

The rules live in a core with no DOM in it at all — no rendering, no browser,
just the die, the grid, and what happens when they meet. The renderer is the
disposable half. When this goes to Godot, the core walks over unchanged and the
pretty part goes in the bin where it belongs.

It's a die. It's a grid. How hard could it be?
